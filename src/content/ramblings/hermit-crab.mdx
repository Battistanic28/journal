# {frontmatter.title}

<br />
Several years ago a close friend of mine shared a story from her childhood that
has managed to continuously resurface in my mind ever since. It goes like this,
<br />
When she was a little girl, my friend and her sisters had a collection of pet
hermit crabs. The terrestrial variety with painted shells that you could buy at
the mall back in the 90’s. Hermit crabs are not the most engaging pet, but they
used to find entertainment in bringing the hermit crabs out to their sandbox in
the backyard and letting them scuttle around the sand castles and channels they
would make for them.
<br />
One day, while collecting their hermit crabs after one such play session, they
realized one was missing! The girls ran to get their mom who helped them search
the back yard in vain. It had wandered off and had managed to get itself lost in
the nearby bushes or god knows where before they noticed it was missing. The
hermit crab was gone :(
<br />
Months passed, almost a year in fact. The girls had forgotten all about the
tragedy of the lost hermit crab and had moved on with their lives. One
afternoon, while moving around some boxes in the basement, their father
discovered a large and hideous bug! However, upon closer inspection he realized
this was no bug at all. It was the lost hermit crab, missing its shell and
dragging along clumps of old spider web! In its time in the wild the crab had
found its way into the basement, and managed to survive well enough on whatever
insects it could prey upon. It had not only managed to survive, it had
apparently thrived well enough to outgrow its old painted shell. It had become
an unrecognizable creature, like Buck in Call of the Wild. No longer a
domesticated thing, no this crab had “been in the s\*\*t “ so to speak. It was
now a wild beast that had transcended the soft existence of an enclosed
habitat..
<br />
The girls were excited about the discovery, and with a freshly cleared
conscience, they went to the mall and found the lost crab, a new, appropriately
sized shell. Once settled into the new shell they returned the crab to its old
home alongside its former colleagues. They did not know that this crab had
become a lion, and they were placing it amongst lambs. Within several days, it
managed to devour all the other hermit crabs. Yes, it ate them… absolutely
metal.
<br />
What is the moral of this story? I’m not sure there is one, but I think it makes
a good anecdote. We are in the middle of a very challenging job market. Many
wildly talented individuals have lost jobs by no fault of their own. In their
job hunting they have done all the things that have guaranteed success in the
past only to find that their environment has changed. It is a brand new
landscape out there with new rules and no playbook. Folks who are unemployed
right now and navigating this brave new world are becoming hardened and powerful
in pursuit of their survival. They are adapting out of necessity. I want to be
very careful with this metaphor, I am not saying that people who have spent
months unemployed before returning to work are going to be wildly aggressive and
eat their colleagues! What I am saying is that people who have struggled to find
meaningful employment in the current job market have needed to develop an
incredible level of grit, resilience and tenacity that can only emerge from a
survival instinct kicking in. For me, navigating a challenging job market and
facing constant rejection stripped every ounce of complacency from my soul and
positioned me to do the best work of my career when I finally landed a new gig.
So here’s a note for anyone hiring right now, do not view an employment gap as a
red flag. Go on, hire a hermit crab today!
