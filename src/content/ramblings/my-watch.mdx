# {frontmatter.title}

<br />
I have a watch. I bought it before my wedding last year. It is not a
particularly expensive watch as far as watches go, nor is it flashy, or rich
with features. It is a simple watch that I enjoy putting on my wrist every
morning. I am not a materialistic person and I put a lot of consideration into
the purchase I make. My watch to me is a symbol of everything I value in a piece
of hardware.
<br />
**It is durable**. Its band and casing is made of titanium with a sapphire crystal,
making it resistant to scratches and scuffing. It is somehow light yet
substantial simultaneously. It is perfectly fitting in a way that feels like a
natural extension of my body. I can wear it to a fancy restaurant, but I feel
equally comfortable having it on while working in my garden.
<br />
**It is low maintenance**. My watch is waterproof, and the titanium body means I can
give it a good scrub with just soap and water when it gets dirty. It is solar
powered, meaning that it will always keep ticking as long as it’s not left in
total darkness for an extended period of time.
<br />
**It does one thing, exceptionally well**. My watch tells the time. It tells the
month and day of the week to boot. That is all it does, and it does its job
predictably and reliably in a way that adds an aesthetic value to the most
mundane of tasks.
<br />
Compare this to most products available to us these days. In an age where weekly
firmware updates and agreement to ever evolving “terms of service” are
prerequisite to our use. My watch cannot be bricked when a new model comes out,
my watch can not spy on me or sell my data. I cannot be prosecuted for modifying
my watch or investigating its inner workings. Outside of the lifetime warranty,
my watch’s relationship with its manufacturer was completely severed at time of
purchase. It doesn’t do these things because I’ve “opted out”, it is physically
incapable of these things by nature of its design.
<br />
Durability, simplicity, reliability, privacy, these are the “features” I look
for in modern devices. They are becoming harder to come by, but they do exist
are worth seeking out when choosing the things we own.
