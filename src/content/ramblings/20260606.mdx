# A case for optimism

<br />
My brother sent me a photo the other day. Mixed in with a fleet of rusted and
battle tested commercial fishing vessels, an absolute behemoth of a luxury yacht
moored in Seattle’s Lake Union. “Mark Zuckerberg’s super yacht” his caption
read.
<br />
The mental image of a historical battleship sending an 18lb cannonball through
the hull of that monstrosity somehow gave a sense of inner peace and I had to
stop and consider why. You see, the appearance of Mark Zuckerberg’s super yacht
in Seattle immediately followed the latest round of Meta layoffs. Approximately
8,000 total, including the termination of 1,500 Seattle based employees.
<br />
Now it is one thing to lose a job unexpectedly. However, having your livelihood
stripped only to find a symbol of the grotesque wealth that your labor helped
produce parked in front of your face should be enough to make one's blood boil
over. Was Zuckerberg even in town? Who knows. Is he really that disconnected and
tone deaf? Probably. Either way the message is received loud and clear. “I do
this, because I can… and also f… you.”
<br />
I want to be careful not to victimize tech workers too much, there aren’t too
many folks in this industry living paycheck to paycheck, However, an income gap,
even if it doesn’t lead to immediate financial ruin, sends ripple effects that
if timed poorly, can dramatically shift the course of an individual’s life. If a
young couple loses an income stream while trying for kids they may choose to
delay until their financial situation stabilizes again. Depending on the timing,
they may miss their window of opportunity. This is one example.
<br />
Every time there is a mass layoff like this I see a million posts made in
solidarity with the workers. Solidarity is nice in difficult times, it feels
good to blame “the Man” together, but I’d like to present a more optimistic
outlook here. For the past 10 years or so big tech has hoarded some of the
brightest minds in our generation. Withholding talent from smaller firms, other
industries and even universities. What happens when you release that wave of
talent back into the world? Time will tell, but I think it’s unlikely we’ll have
tens of thousands of tech workers permanently unemployed or pivoting to careers
in the trades. Rather I see every laid off employee as a little spark of talent
in a field of grass kept dry for the last decade. And the thing about a spark is
--- it only takes one to start a fire. With all this talent headed for companies
outside the “magnificent 7” and potentially into other industries entirely, we
may see the unleashing of a torrent of innovation. With any luck we may see
masses of incredible human beings applying their talents to problems beyond
perfecting the dark arts of advertising, perhaps leading to the demise of the
tech monopolies in power today. We have the numbers behind us to decide the
future, YOU ARE NOT POWERLESS and don’t ever forget that.
