# A case for optimism

My brother sent me a photo the other day. Mixed in with a fleet of rusted and battle tested commercial fishing vessels, an absolute behemoth of a luxury yacht moored in Seattle’s Lake Union. “Mark Zuckerberg’s super yacht” his caption read.

<br />
My immediate reaction was where can I get my hands on a pirate ship? The mental
image of sending an 18lb cannonball through the hull of that monstrosity somehow
gave a sense of inner peace and I had to stop and consider why. You see, the
appearance of Mark Zuckerberg’s super yacht in Seattle immediately followed the
latest round of Meta layoffs. Approximately 8,000 total, including the
termination of 1,500 Seattle based employees.
<br />
Now it is one thing to lose a job unexpectedly. However, having your livelihood
stripped only to find a symbol of the grotesque wealth that your labor helped
produce parked in front of your face should be enough to make one's blood boil
over. Was Zuck even in town? Who knows. Is he really that disconnected and tone
deaf? Probably. Either way the message is received loud and clear. _“I do this,
because I can… and also fuck you.”_
<br />
I myself have been a casualty in the tech layoff fad. I don’t want to victimize
tech workers too much, there aren’t too many folks in this industry living
paycheck to paycheck, However, an income gap, even if it doesn’t lead to
immediate financial ruin, sends ripple effects that if timed poorly, can
dramatically shift the course of an individual’s life. If a young couple loses
an income stream while trying for kids they may choose to delay until their
financial situation stabilizes again. Depending on the timing, they may miss
their window of opportunity. This is one example.
<br />
_[Big tech executives chime in here with a reminder that they offered services
to freeze your eggs…]_
<br />
In the year 2026 you can’t open your computer, turn on your tv, step into a
coffee shop or spend a weekend backpacking with your friends without the topic
of ai bulldozing it’s way into every goddamn conversation. As a technology, it’s
exciting, it’s useful, but it’s exhausting. Let’s move on please. In the past
few years tech executives have kidnapped an army of venture capitalists and
strapped them all into chairs. Right now there are warehouses full of them,
electrodes connected to the reward centers of their brains, providing them with
a dopamine kick every time they push the big red button to invest more money.
Eyelids twitching, mouth foaming, they don’t even like it anymore, but they
can’t stop.
<br />
Now if there is one thing that a robber baron loves even more than free money,
it’s unpaid labor, and that is the promise of ai thus far. All of the wealth
generating productivity without the human inconveniences of sleep, personal time
and paychecks. We can argue whether this is the current state of the world we
live in. I think it is probably not, but even if it is the future we’re
barreling towards, I’d argue there is still time to decide whether we want this.
What we do know however, is that big tech dramatically overhired during the
years of “The Great Resignation”. Did a company whose best product (in my humble
opinion) is Craigslist with a more modern UI need to top out at 80,000+
employees? I think not, and of course tech executives knew that they had more
people than jobs. It was hoarding, plain and simple. Hoarding talent from
competition. Well the bill has come due, and it is no longer practical.
Efficiency gains from ai may be partially responsible, but it’s also an
incredibly convenient scapegoat.
<br />
Every time there is a mass layoff like this I see a million posts made in
solidarity with the workers. Solidarity is nice in difficult times, it feels
good to blame “the Man” together, but I’d like to present a more optimistic
outlook here. For the past 10 years or so big tech has hoarded some of the
brightest minds in our generation. Withholding talent from smaller firms, other
industries and even universities. What happens when you release that wave of
talent back into the world? Time will tell, but I think it’s unlikely we’ll have
tens of thousands of tech workers permanently unemployed or pivoting to careers
in the trades. Rather I see every laid off employee as a little spark of talent
in a field of grass kept dry for the last decade. And the thing about a spark is
\- it only takes one to start a fire. With all this talent headed for companies
outside the “magnificent 7” and potentially into other industries entirely, we
may see the unleashing of a torrent of innovation. With any luck we may see
masses of incredible human beings applying their talents to problems beyond
perfecting the dark arts of advertising, perhaps leading to the demise of the
tech monopolies in power today. We have the numbers behind us to decide the
future, YOU ARE NOT POWERLESS and don’t ever forget that. Cannons at the ready
comrades, your time is now.
